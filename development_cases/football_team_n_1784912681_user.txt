SECTION 1 — EXECUTIVE BRIEFING (STORY MODE)
------------------------------------------------------------
The Metropolitan Lions, a storied but financially struggling football franchise, stood at a crossroads. Founded in 1923, the team had a passionate fan base but had not won a championship in over two decades. Their current stadium, though historic, was outdated and lacked the modern amenities that top-tier sponsors demanded. Team owner Eleanor Vance had inherited the team from her father and was determined to restore its glory without sacrificing its identity.

The Lions' financial situation had deteriorated over the past five years. Rising player salaries, coupled with declining merchandise sales and stadium revenue, had eroded their profit margins. Their existing sponsorship deal with MetroBank was set to expire, and the bank had indicated they would not renew at current rates. Vance knew she needed to secure a substantial new sponsorship to keep the team competitive and fund much-needed stadium renovations.

Across town, GlobalTech Industries, a rapidly growing technology conglomerate, was looking to expand its brand presence in the sports market. CEO Marcus Chen had identified the Lions as an ideal partner due to their loyal fan base and prime television exposure. Chen believed that associating GlobalTech with the Lions would enhance the company's image as innovative and community-focused. However, he was concerned about the team's on-field performance and the potential for negative press if the Lions continued to struggle.

The negotiations between Vance and Chen had begun tentatively, with both sides holding firm on their initial positions. Vance emphasized the team's heritage and passionate fan base, arguing that these intangible assets justified a premium sponsorship fee. Chen countered with data showing the team's declining viewership and merchandise sales, suggesting a more moderate approach. The stadium's outdated facilities further complicated matters, as GlobalTech was seeking modern digital integration opportunities that the current venue couldn't support.

As the negotiations progressed, both sides began to explore creative solutions. Vance proposed phased stadium renovations that could be funded partially by the sponsorship deal, while Chen suggested a multi-year contract with performance-based incentives. The discussions touched on sensitive topics like brand visibility, exclusivity clauses, and the potential for player endorsements. Each variable carried different weight for both parties, with Vance prioritizing immediate financial stability and Chen seeking maximum brand exposure and long-term association with a winning team.

The clock was ticking. The Lions' season was approaching, and both Vance and Chen knew that a successful deal would not only secure the team's immediate future but also position it for sustained success. The challenge lay in finding common ground on five critical variables: the base price of the sponsorship, the level of brand visibility, the exclusivity of the deal, the contract duration, and the structure of performance bonuses. Each decision carried financial implications that would impact both organizations' bottom lines for years to come.
