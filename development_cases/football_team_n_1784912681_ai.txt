SECTION 1 — EXECUTIVE BRIEFING (STORY MODE)
------------------------------------------------------------
GlobalTech Industries had been methodically expanding its brand presence in the sports market over the past three years. Under the leadership of CEO Marcus Chen, the technology conglomerate had successfully sponsored several high-profile events and athletes, but lacked a deep association with a major sports franchise. Chen believed that partnering with a football team would provide the sustained brand exposure his company needed to compete with established tech giants in the consumer market.

The Metropolitan Lions, despite their storied history, had been struggling financially and on the field. Chen saw this as an opportunity—GlobalTech could provide the financial lifeline the team needed while positioning itself as a savior of the franchise. The company's marketing team had identified the Lions' passionate fan base as a valuable demographic for GlobalTech's expanding consumer products division. However, they were concerned about the team's recent performance and the potential for negative brand association if the Lions continued to lose.

Chen had authorized his negotiation team, led by CFO Sarah Jenkins, to pursue a comprehensive sponsorship deal that would maximize GlobalTech's brand visibility and control. The team had been monitoring the Lions' situation for months and knew that the team's current stadium lacked modern digital capabilities that GlobalTech was eager to leverage. This presented both an opportunity and a challenge—GlobalTech could fund renovations but would need significant concessions to justify the investment.

As negotiations began, Jenkins emphasized GlobalTech's desire for maximum brand exposure and a long-term partnership. She presented data showing that the Lions' television ratings, while down from previous years, still reached millions of viewers weekly. She also highlighted the team's social media presence as an untapped resource for digital marketing. The Lions' counteroffers focused on financial terms and brand protection, creating an immediate tension between GlobalTech's desire for visibility and the team's need to preserve its identity.

The negotiations had reached a critical juncture. Jenkins knew that GlobalTech's board was expecting a substantial return on investment, and she needed to secure terms that would justify the sponsorship expense. The five key variables—base price, brand visibility, exclusivity, contract duration, and performance bonuses—each carried different weight for GlobalTech. Jenkins prioritized brand exposure above all else, as it directly aligned with the company's marketing objectives. However, she also needed to ensure the deal was financially viable and protected GlobalTech from potential risks associated with the team's uncertain performance.
